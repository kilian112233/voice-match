ok so first the optics. if you wanna ID someone at 100m, johnson criteria. for identification not just detection you want like 8 line pairs per meter. a person's roughly half a meter wide. so at 100m that's 5 mrad, and you need about 13 pixels across them. bump that up and it's 26 px per meter.

for a 1280 wide sensor that works out to a pretty narrow fov. like 28 degrees. which honestly is not that zoomed in. kinda caught me off guard.

thermal is where it gets rough. lepton 3.5 is only 160x120, even the 320x240 version. you'd need like a 7 degree fov to get those 13 pixels on a person at 100m. that's a really long lens for a tiny sensor.

but wait you said night vision with IR illuminators. that's not thermal, that's just a normal camera with ir light on it. so we're looking at nir, not a lepton. with a 1080p sensor it's actually fine, 42 degree fov covers it. wide angle doesn't even need zoom.

the catch is you want identification, not just spotting them. nato has a stricter standard, stanag 4559, about 6 cycles across the target and covering 150% of it. that changes the numbers a good bit.
